\section{Note S1: Hyperparameter sweep and bge-m3 model}

Figure~\ref{fig:sweeps-e5} presents the cross-validated $R^2$ for all elastic net models reported in the main paper for different numbers of PCA components of the \emph{multilingual-e5-large} embeddings that are entered as predictors. \change{These results are based on a single (non-repeated, non-nested) \res{set.folds}-fold cross-validation run, as repeating the nested cross-validation for every number of components would have been computationally prohibitive. As the hyperparameters are selected on the same folds that are used for evaluation here, these $R^2$ values tend to be optimistic compared to the nested results in the main text \synthmismatch{(hardly in the APEQ data, but more clearly in the GCMQ data and especially in the comparatively small PTSD dataset)}; the informative part is therefore the comparison \emph{across} numbers of components. \synthmismatch{In the APEQ and GCMQ data, $R^2$ increases steeply up to about 10--15 components and remains largely stable up to about 40 components before slowly decreasing;} the \res{set.nprin} components used in the main analysis lie within this stable range. For PCL-5 in the PTSD data, the pattern is similar but noisier. For PTGI, no number of components leads to an increase over the baseline model.} Figure~\ref{fig:sweeps-bge} presents the same information for the \emph{bge-m3} embeddings, where we find \synthmismatch{\change{comparable results, with one exception: for PCL-5, $R^2$ continues to increase up to 40--60 components, so that the main analysis with \res{set.nprin} components is, if anything, conservative here (although the gains at larger numbers of components, estimated on fewer than 210 training participants, are particularly prone to the optimism described above)}}. These results for the \emph{bge-m3} analysis are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:results_summary_bge}, in the same format as Table~1 of the main text for the main  \emph{multilingual-e5-large} analysis.

\section{Note S3: Available variables not used in the analyses}

% describes only the original data: red, as \synthmismatch (which cannot span paragraphs)
{\color{red}The original studies assessed more variables than those entering our analyses (see the Method section of the main text for the variables that were used, and the original publications for the complete set of measures). The following main groups of available variables were not used. Not listed are survey metadata (e.g., completion times), the individual items of questionnaires that entered the analyses as scale or factor scores, and free-text fields other than the analysed responses.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{APEQ:} country of residence; lifetime diagnoses of mental disorders; time elapsed since the experience; clarity of the memory of the experience; route of administration; concomitant use of other substances; setting categories (e.g., retreat, ceremonial, party, therapeutic, or nature setting), number of people present, presence of a guide or sitter, music, and suitability of the setting; psychedelic use before and after the experience; ratings of the impact of the experience; items of the Altered States of Consciousness Rating Scale and of the Psychological Insight Scale; the APEQ subscales on introspection and interaction.
	\item \textbf{GCMQ:} country of residence; time elapsed since the experience; route of administration; number of previous uses of classical psychedelics; number of people present and presence of a supporting person; further single items on demographic characteristics, the setting, and drug use; a nine-item symptom checklist; the APEQ subscales on introspection and interaction; the GCMQ relationship subscale (GCMQ-RE; see the main text) and a supplementary GCMQ scale.
	\item \textbf{PTSD:} time elapsed since the experience; strength of the acute effects; concomitant use of other substances; setting categories, number of people present, and presence and characteristics of a trip sitter; substance use before and after the experience; the type of challenging experience; earlier stressful life events (LEC-5) and related memories; pre-existing diagnoses of mental disorders; the APEQ subscales on introspection and interaction; variables assessed with reference to the time after the experience (persisting perceptual symptoms, diagnoses after the experience, and treatment attempts).
\end{itemize}}
